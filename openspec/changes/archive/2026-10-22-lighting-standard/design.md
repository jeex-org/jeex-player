# Design

## Context

The player's lighting lives inside `RenderRuntime` (`buildLights`, `defaultEnvironment`): ACES Filmic at exposure 1.0, `RoomEnvironment` through PMREM at intensity 0.5, a hemisphere light, a shadow-casting sun with a fixed frustum and a fill. Swatches (`swatch-preview.ts`) light with an ambient, a key and a fill and read an 8-bit target without tone mapping; default-state captures clone the runtime's named lights; the development furniture gallery has a fourth rig. `three` is a devDependency bundled into every entry of the package.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:** one standard (display transform, calibration, environment, two rigs) used by every player path and published for other applications; three.js r186.

**Non-Goals:** fitted sun, ceiling shadow casting, probes, HDRI, post-processing, quality tiers, mirror capture under render targets, a stricter WebGL 2 probe. They come later and will change the rig values; consumers follow by updating the package.

## Decisions

### 1. Injected engine, bound once

`createLightingContext(three, renderer)` takes the caller's three.js namespace and renderer. The module (`src/lighting/`) has type-only imports of three and no DOM access, so its published entry carries no engine and every object it builds belongs to the caller's engine and renderer. The namespace is described by a narrow `LightingThree` type (the constructors and constants actually used), and the player passes a narrow object too, so bundling keeps tree shaking.

Alternatives: three as an external peer dependency (imposes a peer on the whole package, still no guarantee of one engine instance); a data-only export (consumers rebuild the rig and drift).

### 2. Revision check before allocation

`three.REVISION !== '186'` throws `LightingError('engine-revision')` naming both revisions, before any texture or target exists. The patch version is pinned by package manifests, not checked at runtime.

### 3. The neutral environment is generated by the package

An equirectangular grey studio, generated on the CPU as linear float RGB (equal channels): a vertical gradient (brighter above, darker below the horizon) plus two soft rectangular panels that give metals a highlight to reflect. It is converted with the caller's `PMREMGenerator.fromEquirectangular`; the context keeps the PMREM render target (not only its texture) and disposes it. The recipe is a pure function, so its pixels are equal in every context and testable without a GPU.

### 4. Calibration through the light

Exposure stays 1.0. PBR Neutral subtracts 0.04 in its uncompressed range, so a linear-0.18 Lambert patch reads 0.18 when its incoming irradiance satisfies E/π = (0.18 + 0.04)/0.18 ≈ 1.222. The reference key is white at intensity 1.0 (contributing 1/π); the environment is scaled by a normalisation computed from the recipe so its cosine-weighted irradiance on the key-facing normal supplies the rest. A unit test integrates the recipe; a browser test reads the rendered patch (three's dielectric specular and energy terms make the analytic figure approximate, so the browser figure decides the frozen constant).

### 5. Two rigs from one context, one rig per scene

`LightingRig` = `attach(scene)`, `detach()`, `dispose()`. A module-level `WeakMap<scene, rig>`, shared by every context built from the same copy of the module, enforces one rig per scene (the player's bundles and the published entry are separate copies, each with its own map). Attaching saves `environment`, `environmentIntensity` and `environmentRotation`, sets the standard's (identity rotation), and adds the rig's lights in one group; detaching removes the group and restores each saved value only where the scene still holds the value the rig set. A context keeps a set of its rigs; disposing it disposes them, then the PMREM target. After disposal every factory and `attach` throws `disposed`. Construction allocates the source texture and PMREM generator inside `try/finally`, so a failure releases them.

| | Reference | Apartment |
|---|---|---|
| Environment intensity | calibrated (decision 4): 0.543 | 1.8 × the calibrated value |
| Key / sun | white 1.0, from upper front right, no shadow | `#fff4e6` 3.0, the former position and target, PCF shadow (default 2048), ±13 m, bias −0.0002, normal bias 0.02 |
| Hemisphere | none | `#fffcf6` / `#6d675f` 0.9 |
| Fill | none | `#bed7de` 0.4, no shadow |

The apartment numbers were found by comparing three fixed tour views (a living room, a kitchen, a bedroom with a window) rendered with the former ACES rig and with candidate Neutral rigs. Neutral keeps hue where ACES washes it out, so the former warm sun and sky tinted white furniture cream; their colours were made less saturated, and the environment, which is a darker grey studio than the former synthetic room, was raised until the views matched in brightness. The sun was then lowered for the opening dollhouse view, where a large ground plane in full sun otherwise reads brighter and warmer than before; Neutral shows that plane's own beige where ACES had washed it towards white. Options are validated by a Zod mini schema in `lighting-options.schema.ts` (strict object, `shadowMapSize` a power of two 256…4096) before anything is allocated; the size is then lowered to `renderer.capabilities.maxTextureSize` when smaller.

### 5a. Errors and prerequisites

`LightingError` (exported) carries a code: `engine-revision`, `colour-management`, `bad-options`, `rig-attached`, `scene-has-rig`, `disposed`. The context requires `three.ColorManagement.enabled` and a linear sRGB working space and never changes those globals, since other viewers on the page share them. The context sets the renderer's tone mapping, exposure, output colour space and `shadowMap.enabled`/`type = PCFShadowMap`.

### 5b. Lost GPU context

The module installs no listeners. A consumer recovers by: stop rendering on `webglcontextlost`; on `webglcontextrestored` dispose the old context (its rigs go with it) and create a new context and rigs for the surviving scenes. The player already remounts its runtime on restore, which does exactly that. This sequence goes into the README section.

### 6. Player integration

- `RenderRuntime` creates one context and one apartment rig on construction, disposes both, and recreates them on a context restore (the runtime is remounted then). `runtime.lights` is removed; its one production reader, default-state capture, creates its own apartment rig through the runtime's context.
- Swatches attach a reference rig to their preview scene, render into a half-float target and pass through the existing display pass (`displayMaterial` / `drawDisplayPass`), so a swatch is tone mapped exactly once.
- The development furniture gallery uses the reference rig.
- Sky gradient and fog are not tone mapped by three live (sRGB background, fog mixed after the transform), while the still and panorama display pass transforms the whole frame. That difference exists today and is kept; the spec states it, and a test checks that a lit surface agrees between a still and the live view.
- Lamp models from the furniture bank carry their own point lights; swatches and the gallery leave them as they are (they are the content, not the rig).

### 7. Publishing

A new library build pass `lighting` (ES, entry `src/lighting/index.ts`, three marked external as a type-only safety net) emits `dist-lib/jeex-viewer-lighting.js` and its declarations; `package.json` gains `"./lighting"`. A post-build test walks the emitted lighting entry and every chunk it imports and asserts that none contains three.js or refers to `document`/`window`; a consumer fixture imports `jeex-viewer/lighting` by package name and typechecks against the emitted declarations; `knip` and the budget check know the new entry, and the viewer's 700 KB budget is re-measured. `LIGHTING_STANDARD_VERSION = 1`; it rises with every intentional change of look and is meant for cache keys of previews.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [The demo looks different] → before/after screenshots of three fixed tour views at r185/ACES, r186/ACES and r186/Neutral; an update run of the pixel baselines rewrote none, so they are kept.
- [r186 itself changes PBR energy and PMREM blur] → the middle screenshot stage isolates it.
- [A consumer mixes engines] → the revision check and documentation; objects are always built with the caller's namespace.
- [Calibration drift] → unit integration test plus a browser pixel test of a fixed fixture (standard material, base colour linear 0.18, roughness 1, metalness 0, normal and camera along the key direction, mean of the central 4 × 4 pixels, ±0.0036).
- [Materials other than grey] → a reference sheet (grey, white, black, polished and brushed metal, saturated red and blue, a sheen fabric) rendered under the reference rig for eye review; not asserted numerically.
- [Environment parameters drift] → resolution, orientation, gradient and panel parameters are part of `LIGHTING_STANDARD` and frozen with version 1.
