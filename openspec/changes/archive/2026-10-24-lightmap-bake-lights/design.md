# Design

## Context

The bake tool builds an apartment's light bake offline from the shell file the player exports (`exportShellForBake`). It needs the lights too, in the units the live view draws them with, so that the baked indirect light and the baked lamp light match what the player shows next to the live direct sun. The numbers live in three places of the player: the lighting standard (`src/lighting/lighting-standard.ts`), the sun fitting from a scene's `site` (`sunDirection`, which `prepareSunShadow` aims the live rig with), and the bank builders that create the lamps (`src/assets/asset-builders-lighting-decor.ts`). The module follows the shell export's pattern: internal, imported from source, Bun without a DOM.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- One function, `bakeLights(scene, options)`, that reads every number from those sources and answers a document judged by its own Zod schema.
- Tests that compare against live objects (the apartment rig built with a stub renderer, the scene built by `SceneBuilder`), so a change of the live value fails a test if `bakeLights` would not follow.
- The sidecar records which lights a bake took.

**Non-Goals:**
- The HDRI sky that replaces the interim recipe; spot and area lamps; switchable (live) lamps; colour temperatures other than «Original»; any public API.

## Decisions

1. **Units and conventions.** three r186 physical units in the linear Rec.709 working space, glTF world axes. Standard hexes are linearised by `THREE.Color`, as three's lights read them. Irradiance carries no π factor.
2. **Sun.** `direction` is the direction light travels, `-sunDirection(scene.site).direction`, the same convention as the sidecar's `sun`, so the bake tool copies it; `color` is the standard sun colour, `strength` its intensity (W/m² on a facing surface). The walk's interior factor is applied to a lightmap at run time and is never baked. The field names match the sidecar's `sun`.
3. **Sky: two hemispheres split at the horizon.** Uniform radiance `upper` above the horizon and `lower` below, each hemisphere light colour × intensity / π. For a plane tilted θ from up, the share of a sphere's irradiance coming from its upper half is (1 + cos θ) / 2, which is exactly the weight of three's hemisphere light (`mix(ground, sky, 0.5·n·up + 0.5)`), so the split world gives every normal the live hemisphere's irradiance, not only an up-facing one. A smooth gradient would not. The bake tool builds it as a world whose colour switches on the sign of the view direction's z (Blender is +Z up). The environment's diffuse, the walk's ambient floor and the fill light are left out: they stand in for the indirect light the bake computes, and the baked-surface policy drops them on baked shells.
4. **Lamps through one seam.** `SceneBuilder.build()` builds the scene with the given bank, exactly as the live mount does, `updateMatrixWorld`, and `collectLampLights(root)` (`src/render/lamp-lights.ts`) answers the lamp lights: the lights placements declare, `{ placementId, index, position, colour, lumens, intensity, decay, cutoff, shadow }`, placements in scene order then index. `bakeLights` reads lamps only through it. The collector is a stub that answers none today: no object carries a light description yet, and a bank builder's procedural bulb is not a lamp light. Bank bulbs therefore stay live on a baked shell, which is the baked/live lamp rule's safe side (a live lamp may double-light, never fails to darken). The lighting work that introduces light descriptions replaces the collector's body; `bakeLights`' output does not change shape. The collector is the third, defaulted argument so a test can stand in for it.
5. **Lamp fields and watts.** `id` = `placementId`, `index`, `position`, `color` = `colour` (authored, «Original»), `intensity` in candela, and `power = BAKE_LAMP_WATTS_PER_CANDELA × intensity` with the factor 4π named once: a radius-0 point lamp of radiant power P gives P / (4π d²) at distance d, three gives I / d². The conversion is a local function until the lighting module publishes its own, which replaces it. Candela is kept beside the watts so the bake tool's lamp landmark can confirm the factor. The cut-off and the shadow flag are live-only and left out. `(id, index)` is unique.
6. **The bank is required.** `bakeLights(scene, bank)` with `bank` = `SHIPPED_BANK` or `{ catalogue, catalogueLibrary }` (both required, as the live project view is given them) (a project's `projectBank(manifest, baseUrl)` catalogue and `defaultLibrary`), no default: forgetting it would silently build the scene against a bank the live view does not use. A call without one is a type error and throws at run time. `SHIPPED_BANK` passes no catalogue to the scene builder, exactly as a live view without a project. Finish options reach no light and are not taken. three's colour management must be on (the rig refuses without it too); otherwise the standard's hexes would stay sRGB.
7. **No inference.** No lamp is inferred from an id, a category, a builder or a model. A project that redeclares a bank lamp's id as its own object has no light live and none in the bake.
8. **Recipe ids.** `recipe: 'jeex-bake-lights@1'` versions the meaning of every field; `sky.recipe` discriminates the sky, so the later HDRI recipe is a new branch of the schema. `standardVersion` is the lighting standard's appearance version the numbers were read from. These are vocabularies, not ids.
9. **Sidecar `lights`.** `{ recipe, standardVersion, skyRecipe }`, strict, recipe literals taken from the bake-lights schema. Optional in the schema (a calibration room has its own lights), required by the apartment check (`verifiedSidecar`), like `shellInputsHash`. The sidecar's `sky` is the bake's own record, not judged by the player (under this recipe `{ upper, lower }`). The player does not compare `standardVersion` with its own version today: whether a bake made under another appearance version is refused is a policy of its own, not part of recording it.
10. **Into the sidecar.** The schema's documentation states the mapping: the sidecar's `sun` is `bakeLights.sun` verbatim, its `sky` is `{ upper, lower }`, its `lights` is `{ recipe, standardVersion, skyRecipe: sky.recipe }`, and its `lamps` lists the placement ids of the baked lamps, each distinct id once; baking or keeping live is decided per placement, for all its lights together. The sidecar's `sun` stays in the glTF frame. Positions, the sun and the sky axis are in the shell file's frame, converted the same way as the shell. The stand-in sidecar of the tests lists no lamps (every lamp live), which recipe `@1` allows.
12. **The shell export names its finishes.** `exportShellForBake(scene, { mintId, finishes })` with `finishes` = `SHIPPED_FINISHES` or `projectFinishes(manifest.library.materials)`. Before, both lookups were optional: giving a project's materials without its lookup kept the input digest (which covers the materials) but painted a surface whose finish id the project answers differently with the bundled finish, so the bake's albedo silently differed from the live view. `projectFinishes` is the live project view's composition, moved to `src/materials` and used by both, so the two cannot diverge.
11. **Determinism.** Plain arithmetic over fixed inputs in the collector's order; two calls give equal documents. Numbers are not rounded: the schema checks `power` against the intensity to 1e-9 and the sun direction to unit length to 1e-9.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- Until placements declare lights, no lamp is baked and bank bulbs stay live: a baked room gets no indirect bounce from its lamps. That is the agreed interim, not a defect.
- On the live view a point light casts no shadow and reaches through walls up to its cut-off; in the bake walls block it and there is no cut-off. Lamp intensities were tuned by eye under the live behaviour, so baked lamp light will look different.
- Baked rooms read darker than today's editing look: the editing view adds the environment's diffuse and a fill light that the bake does not take. This is intended; keeping the editing brightness is a calibration of the sky radiance, not another light.
- The sky reproduces the hemisphere's irradiance for an unoccluded plane; inside rooms the bake adds occlusion the live hemisphere never had. That is the point of the bake.
