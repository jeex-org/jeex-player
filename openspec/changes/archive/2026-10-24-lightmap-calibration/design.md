# Design

## Context

See proposal.md for the motivation. The bake writes UV1 in page coordinates (`atlas` mode), stores E/π per texel (three's lightmap path multiplies by the Lambert BRDF albedo/π, so the intensity is π), and encodes linear light as RGBM with range 8 in lossless WebP, one file per mip level, each level filtered from decoded linear light. three r186 adds `lightMapTexel.rgb * lightMapIntensity` to the diffuse irradiance and ignores alpha, and its `Texture.channel` defaults to 0. The walk lighting patch replaces `#include <lights_fragment_maps>` on every standard material with its own expansion of that chunk.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:** a closed, validated sidecar; exact upload of RGBM levels; a decode that composes with other shader patches; a transactional binding by entity id; measured proof of intensity, colour space, orientation and sun split.

**Non-Goals:** loading a shipped shell by publication (step 2: shell export, entity-named meshes, the publication manifest field); the lighting's suppression of hemisphere, probe grid, ambient, environment diffuse and baked-lamp diffuse on marked materials (delivered by the lighting); furniture probes; KTX2 or HDR containers; normal-map response under baked light; reapplying the decode after the selection highlight or a finish swap clones a material.

## Decisions

1. **Modules** (`src/lightmap/`, a layer right above `render`):
   - `light-bake.schema.ts`: the sidecar schema and its judge, with a hand-written `LightBake` type in `light-bake.ts` proved by a compile-time agreement check;
   - `rgbm.ts`: the reference decode (pure);
   - `lightmap-page.ts`: one page from its WebP level files (fetch, `createImageBitmap` with `premultiplyAlpha: 'none'` and `colorSpaceConversion: 'none'`, size check per level, a `Texture` with `image` = level 0, `mipmaps` = levels 0..lodLimit, `generateMipmaps` false, `LinearMipmapLinearFilter`, `NoColorSpace`, `flipY` false, `channel` 1, anisotropy 1);
   - `lightmap-shader.ts`: the RGBM decode as a material decoration;
   - `lightmap-binding.ts`: the transaction (resolve, check, clone, commit, undo);
   - `light-bake-load.ts`: fetch sidecar and shell, hash the shell, judge, load pages; one entry used by the dev page and the tests.
   `src/render/baked-surface.ts` is the seam the lighting reads; it sits in `render` so the lighting code never imports the lightmap layer.
2. **Decode as a macro.** The decoration appends, right after `#include <lightmap_pars_fragment>`, `#define JEEX_RGBM_RANGE <range>` and `#define lightMapIntensity ( lightMapIntensity * lightMapTexel.a * JEEX_RGBM_RANGE )`. The C++ preprocessor rules of GLSL keep a macro from expanding inside itself, and `lightMapIntensity` is read only beside `lightMapTexel` (`lights_fragment_maps`, `meshbasic`), so the decode works on the include token and on any patch's expanded copy of the maps chunk, in either hook order, and never consumes a token another patch replaces. Alternative rejected: replacing the lightmap line after expanding the maps include, which would remove the token the walk lighting patch looks for when it runs later.
3. **Hook composition.** The decode never assigns hooks: it calls `addShaderTransform(material, 'jeex-lightmap-rgbm-r<range>', 1, transform)` from `src/render/baked-surface.ts`, the lighting's seam. The stub there chains: the previous `onBeforeCompile` runs first with the material as `this`, the previous key is kept (three's default key, which returns the current hook's source, is captured before wrapping) and `|id@version` is appended. The lighting's composer later replaces the stub, expanding its chunks once and applying box-probe, baked and RGBM transforms in that order. Anchor: the `#include <lightmap_pars_fragment>` token if present (left in place), else the expanded line `uniform float lightMapIntensity;`. A material decorated once is not decorated again with another range (a private mark on the material).
4. **One texture per page, one clone per (material, page).** In atlas mode the texture transform is the identity, so every clone of a page shares the page's texture; the GPU upload happens once per context. A clone copies the source's shader hooks before the decoration, since `Material.clone` does not.
5. **Supported materials and preflight.** Only a single `MeshStandardMaterial` (physical included) per bound mesh; a transmissive one is left unbound and named; the clone drops any AO map. Because compilation is lazy, the binding runs each prepared clone's composed hook, with the clone's final state as `this`, on a throwaway copy of the physical shader and refuses before committing anything when it throws or the anchor is gone, disposing the prepared clones.
6. **Transaction.** `bindLightBake(root, bake)` resolves every binding to exactly one mesh by `name` (refusing groups, multi-material meshes, and missing, non-finite or non-2D UV1, or UV1 outside the rect by more than half a texel), resolves the lamps, prepares every clone, and only then assigns materials and lamp flags. It answers `{ unresolvedLamps, unbind }`. `unbind` restores a mesh only while it still holds its clone (a later finish change wins), restores lamp flags, disposes clones. Pages belong to the loaded bake (`loadLightBake` answers `{ bake, pages, dispose }`): `dispose` disposes each texture and closes each bitmap once; a page load that fails part-way closes the bitmaps it decoded, and a later page's failure disposes the pages already loaded.
7. **Fixture.** `tests/fixtures/lightmap-calibration/` holds the calibration bake: `shell.glb`, `sidecar.json`, `lightmap-level0..9.webp`. Two edits from the producer's output, both recorded in `calibration-facts.ts`: the GLB's generator string is neutral (and `shellHash` recomputed for those exact bytes), the sidecar's device list is neutral, and `lamps` lists the corner lamp. `calibration-facts.ts` also holds the scene facts the tests need (ids by role, albedos, the lamp's placement id and position, the window corners) and the golden texels of levels 0..3 decoded independently with libwebp, with SHA-256 digests of each decoded level.
8. **Measurement.** A bare `THREE.Scene`, an orthographic camera, a `FloatType` render target read with `readRenderTargetPixels` (linear, no tone mapping). The intensity and sun-split tests swap the plate's and floor's clones for a zero-specular `MeshPhysicalMaterial` (metalness 0, `specularIntensity` 0) carrying the same lightmap and decode, so the Lambert equation is exact; a standard material is compared with a CPU-decoded float lightmap of the same page. The live sun is a `DirectionalLight` along the sidecar's sun with shadows from the shell; samples are taken inside the window's footprint eroded by 0.15 m and away from the block. Metrics: the plate's mean over its pixels away from its edge (4 texels) gates at 3 %; the landmark's distance gates at 0.15 m; the sun delta's mean over the footprint samples gates at 2 % (four renders) and 3 % (analytic); the standard material against the float reference gates on the mean relative error over floor pixels at 0.5 % and the 95th percentile at 2.5 % (the producer measured RGBM filtering up to 2.1 %). Diagnostics printed but not gating: chart-edge contamination per level 0..3 (each chart's 1-texel edge ring against its 3-texel interior ring, relative) and the footprint against its ring (the footprint grown 1.8 times about its centre) with the sun off.
9. **Dev page.** `dev/lightmap-calibration.html` loads the fixture through `light-bake-load.ts`, binds it onto the shell with a live lamp in a placement group, and offers views: lit scene (orbit), raw RGBM atlas, decoded atlas, lightmap on/off, sun on/off; it prints the measured plate factor, landmark error and sun delta. Development only: not in the build's page list.
10. **Future public shape (not added now).** In the publication manifest, beside the scene and library resources: `lightBake?: { url: string; shell: string }`, the sidecar and the shell GLB relative to the manifest; absent means live lighting.

11. **Live policy vocabulary.** The player's contract keeps `direct sun`, `specular` and `live-lamp light` and disables `baked-lamp diffuse`, `fill` (the apartment rig's fill light), `hemisphere`, `ambient`, `probe-grid diffuse` and `environment diffuse`. The bake side's current vocabulary (`lamp diffuse`, no `fill`, no live lamps) and its sidecar without `lamps` are refused; it changes its schema, emission and tests to match.

## Risks / Trade-offs

1. [The lighting's walk patch assigns hooks and would drop the decode if it runs after binding] → the lighting side composes its hook (its half of the seam); until then the calibration runs without the walk patch, and the report names it.
2. [Hardware filtering of RGBM bytes is not filtering of light] → the producer measured about 0.1 % mean error; accepted for this step.
3. [At mip level 3 an 8-texel gutter can mix neighbouring charts] → measured on the calibration and reported to the producer.
4. [Selection highlight and finish swaps clone or replace materials without the decode] → the calibration scene is static; a shared way to reapply decorations is step 2 work.
5. [A shell AO map would darken baked light twice; normal maps do not change baked light] → known limits, not addressed here.
