# lighting-standard Specification

## Purpose
One lighting standard for every application that renders JEEX content: the same tone curve, exposure, colour handling, environment and light rigs, published by the player package so a material looks the same in asset review, store previews and the player.

## Requirements

### Requirement: Renderer settings

Creating a lighting context SHALL set the renderer to PBR Neutral tone mapping, exposure 1.0, sRGB output and shadow maps enabled with PCF filtering. It SHALL NOT change three.js's global colour management.

#### Scenario: Renderer settings
- **WHEN** a lighting context is created for a renderer
- **THEN** the renderer's tone mapping is PBR Neutral, its exposure is 1.0, its output colour space is sRGB, and its shadow map is enabled with PCF filtering

#### Scenario: Apartment shadows on a fresh renderer
- **WHEN** a context and an apartment rig are created for a newly constructed renderer
- **THEN** the sun casts shadows without the caller changing any renderer setting

### Requirement: Colour management prerequisite

A lighting context SHALL be created only when the caller's three.js has colour management enabled with linear sRGB as its working colour space; otherwise creation SHALL fail with the `colour-management` error before any resource is allocated.

#### Scenario: Colour management off
- **WHEN** a context is requested while the caller's three.js has colour management disabled
- **THEN** creation throws a lighting error with code `colour-management` and no texture or render target was created

### Requirement: Display transform of the player's images

Every lit surface the player draws (live view, still, panorama, swatch) SHALL be tone mapped with PBR Neutral and encoded to sRGB exactly once. Offscreen images apply that transform to the whole frame, so a background, fog or content marked as not tone mapped differs between a capture and the live view, as it does today.

#### Scenario: Swatch through the display transform
- **WHEN** the player renders a material swatch
- **THEN** it renders into a high-dynamic-range target and one display pass applies PBR Neutral, the renderer's exposure and the sRGB encoding

#### Scenario: Still of a lit surface
- **WHEN** a still is captured of a lit surface that the live view also shows
- **THEN** the surface's pixel values in the still and in the live view agree within 2 %

### Requirement: Calibration

Under the reference rig, the calibration fixture SHALL read linear 0.18 after the display transform, within ±0.0036 (2 % relative). The fixture is a flat square of a standard physically based material, linear base colour 0.18, roughness 1, metalness 0, whose normal points at the key light, seen by a camera along the same normal; the reading is the mean of the central 4 × 4 pixels.

#### Scenario: Grey patch under the reference rig
- **WHEN** the calibration fixture is rendered under the reference rig and the central pixels are read and decoded from sRGB to linear
- **THEN** their mean is 0.18 ± 0.0036

### Requirement: Neutral environment

The standard SHALL provide its own deterministic, colourless environment, generated by the package from fixed parameters, so every consumer reflects and is lit by the same surroundings. Consumers SHALL NOT be able to substitute another environment through the standard's API.

#### Scenario: Same environment everywhere
- **WHEN** the environment is generated twice
- **THEN** both results have equal pixel values

#### Scenario: Colourless
- **WHEN** the environment is generated
- **THEN** every texel has equal red, green and blue values

#### Scenario: Orientation is the standard's
- **WHEN** a rig is attached to a scene whose environment rotation was changed
- **THEN** the scene's environment rotation is the identity while the rig is attached, and the previous rotation returns when it is detached

### Requirement: Reference rig

The reference rig SHALL contribute the neutral environment at the calibrated intensity and exactly one white directional key light of intensity 1.0 without shadows; no room, sky light or fill. It is the rig of material swatches and asset review. Lights inside the content being previewed are the content's own and are left as they are.

#### Scenario: Reference rig contribution
- **WHEN** a reference rig is attached to an empty scene
- **THEN** the scene's environment is the standard environment at the calibrated intensity and the scene contains exactly one directional light, white, of intensity 1.0, not casting shadows

### Requirement: Apartment rig

The apartment rig SHALL contribute the neutral environment at the apartment intensity, a hemisphere light, a shadow-casting directional sun and a shadowless directional fill, with values defined by the standard. It is the rig of the player's scenes, including captures of a scene's default state.

#### Scenario: Apartment rig contribution
- **WHEN** an apartment rig is attached to an empty scene
- **THEN** the scene has the standard environment at the apartment intensity, one hemisphere light, one shadow-casting directional sun and one shadowless directional fill, with the standard's colours, intensities and positions

### Requirement: Rig options

An apartment rig SHALL accept an optional shadow map size: an integer power of two from 256 to 4096, default 2048, lowered to the renderer's maximum texture size when that is smaller. Any other value or any unknown option SHALL fail with the `bad-options` error before anything is allocated or any scene is changed.

#### Scenario: Default size
- **WHEN** an apartment rig is created without options
- **THEN** the sun's shadow map is 2048 × 2048

#### Scenario: Rejected size
- **WHEN** an apartment rig is created with a shadow map size of 0, 1000, 8192, 2048.5, NaN or a string
- **THEN** creation throws a lighting error with code `bad-options` naming the option

#### Scenario: Unknown option
- **WHEN** an apartment rig is created with an option the standard does not define
- **THEN** creation throws a lighting error with code `bad-options`

### Requirement: Engine revision

A lighting context SHALL be created only for three.js revision 186. For any other revision, creation SHALL fail with the `engine-revision` error naming the expected and the supplied revision, before any resource is allocated.

#### Scenario: Wrong revision
- **WHEN** a lighting context is requested with a three.js namespace whose revision is "185"
- **THEN** creation throws a lighting error with code `engine-revision` that names 186 and 185, and no render target or texture was created

### Requirement: Caller's engine and renderer

The public lighting entry SHALL build every object with the three.js namespace and renderer the caller passes in, and SHALL import no three.js and touch no DOM itself; it installs no event listeners.

#### Scenario: Objects from the caller's engine
- **WHEN** a consumer creates a context and a rig with its own three.js namespace
- **THEN** every light, texture and render target in the rig was constructed by that namespace

#### Scenario: No engine in the published entry
- **WHEN** the published lighting entry and every file it imports are inspected
- **THEN** none contains three.js or refers to `document` or `window`

### Requirement: One rig per scene

A scene SHALL carry at most one attached rig. Attaching a rig again to the scene it is attached to SHALL do nothing; attaching it to another scene SHALL fail with `rig-attached`; attaching a rig to a scene that carries a different rig SHALL fail with `scene-has-rig`; detaching a rig that is not attached SHALL do nothing.

#### Scenario: Second rig on one scene
- **WHEN** rig A is attached to a scene and rig B, from the same or another context, is attached to the same scene
- **THEN** the second attachment throws a lighting error with code `scene-has-rig` and the scene is unchanged

#### Scenario: One rig on two scenes
- **WHEN** a rig attached to one scene is attached to another scene
- **THEN** it throws a lighting error with code `rig-attached` and stays on the first scene

#### Scenario: Repeated attach and early detach
- **WHEN** a rig is attached to the same scene twice, or detached before it was ever attached
- **THEN** nothing changes and nothing throws

### Requirement: Environment restore on detach

Detaching a rig SHALL remove its lights and SHALL restore the scene's previous environment, environment intensity and environment rotation, each only when the scene still holds the value the rig set; a value the caller changed meanwhile is kept.

#### Scenario: Plain detach
- **WHEN** a rig is detached from a scene it was attached to
- **THEN** its lights are no longer in the scene and the scene's environment, environment intensity and environment rotation are what they were before attaching

#### Scenario: Caller changed the environment meanwhile
- **WHEN** a caller sets its own environment on a scene while a rig is attached, and the rig is then detached
- **THEN** the scene keeps the caller's environment

### Requirement: Resource ownership and disposal

A context SHALL own its environment and every rig it created; a rig SHALL own its lights and shadow maps. Disposing a rig detaches it first. Disposing a context disposes its rigs, then releases its environment. Disposal SHALL be idempotent and SHALL NOT dispose the caller's renderer, scenes, models or materials. A failed creation SHALL release whatever it had allocated.

#### Scenario: Dispose twice
- **WHEN** a context or a rig is disposed twice
- **THEN** the second call does nothing and throws nothing

#### Scenario: Context disposed before its rigs
- **WHEN** a context is disposed while one of its rigs is attached to a scene
- **THEN** the rig is detached and disposed, the scene's environment is restored, and the environment render target is released

#### Scenario: Use after disposal
- **WHEN** a rig is created from a disposed context, or a disposed rig is attached
- **THEN** it throws a lighting error with code `disposed`

#### Scenario: Two viewers
- **WHEN** two contexts exist for two renderers and one of them is disposed
- **THEN** the other context's rigs keep lighting their scene and its renderer keeps rendering

### Requirement: Recovery after a lost GPU context

The standard SHALL not recover a lost GPU context by itself. After a context is restored, a caller SHALL dispose its old lighting context and create a new one with fresh rigs; the player's render runtime does so itself when its context is restored.

#### Scenario: Player after a context restore
- **WHEN** the player's GPU context is lost and restored
- **THEN** the remounted view renders with a newly created lighting context, and a surface lit only by the environment is not black

### Requirement: Published description and version

The entry SHALL publish a read-only description of the standard (engine revision, display transform, calibration target, environment parameters, rig values) and an appearance version number, raised with every intentional change of the look. The entry SHALL export the lighting error type and its codes.

#### Scenario: Description is read-only
- **WHEN** a consumer tries to change a value of the published description, at any depth
- **THEN** the value does not change

#### Scenario: Version
- **WHEN** a consumer reads the appearance version
- **THEN** it is 1 for this first standard
